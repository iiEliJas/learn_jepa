What am I looking at?

The four views are four random draws from the training augmentation recipe. The projector maps each view to 16-d. The target μ is the mean of the four projector outputs, and the prediction term is the mean squared distance from every view to μ.

μ = (1/V) Σ_v z_v L_pred = (1 / V·K) Σ_v Σ_k (μ_k − z_v,k)² paired: four views of the same image shuffled: view v replaced by another image

μ is not detached in training: gradients flow through the target as well as through each view. The shuffled control keeps the four-view shape but destroys the image correspondence.

Try this

1. Compare A and B. Pick two different classes. The paired loss is usually smaller than the shuffled control for both, but the absolute values depend on how hard the image is to augment consistently.

2. Reshuffle. Press “Reshuffle control” on one card. The shuffled loss jumps when the four views come from unrelated images.

3. Read the matrix. The diagonal is always 1. High off-diagonal cosine means the views of this image landed close together in projector space; low values mean the objective still has work to do.

4. Notice the layout. The local 2-D view is a PCA of only four 16-d points. It is a shadow of the real geometry, useful for seeing μ and the arrows, not for measuring separation.

Why the projector, not the probe?

The probe feature is trained to classify; the projector is trained to make augmented views agree and to match N(0, I) under random slices. That is why the card uses projector outputs: they are the tensors that enter L_pred and SIGReg.

The 16-d projector is deliberately low-dimensional. A class readout is not its job; the 512-d proj_head is what the probe sees.

What the shuffled control proves

If the four vectors were unrelated, the mean μ would sit in the middle of an unrelated cloud and the residual would be larger. The gap between paired and shuffled is the evidence that the model learned view invariance, not just a small embedding norm.
