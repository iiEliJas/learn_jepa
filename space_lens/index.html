Lens settings

Mean-centering subtracts the subset mean from every feature dimension before the cosine comparison. It does not change the model; it changes the question you ask of the representation.

What am I looking at?

One dot is one image. Each of the three panels ranks the other 1,999 test images by cosine similarity in a different representation:

CLS 192-d encoder token, before proj_head head 512-d proj_head, the probe feature proj 16-d projector, L_pred + SIGReg sim(a,b) = aᵀb / (‖a‖ ‖b‖)

The CLS and head features share a large common direction. Raw cosine is high for almost every pair; centering removes that direction before comparing. The projector has no such common direction, so it looks diffuse in a different way: it spends its 16 dimensions on prediction and regularisation, not on class separation.

Try this

1. Raw vs centered. Start with the head space and switch to raw. The mean cosine jumps towards 1 and many neighbours look equally close. Centring makes the ranking depend on the image, not the shared offset.

2. Read the vote. Each panel takes a majority vote of its k neighbours. Compare that vote with the probe's prediction in the banner. A space can retrieve the right class without being the space the classifier reads.

3. Look for overlap. The overlap matrix shows how much the three neighbour lists agree. Shared neighbours are the images the representation considers close for reasons that survive a change of space.

Three spaces, three jobs

The CLS token is the encoder's output before the projection head. It is the representation the ViT blocks actually build, but no label ever touches it directly. The 512-d head is a linear map trained by the LeJEPA objective; the probe reads it, so it is the most semantic of the three. The 16-d projector is the space where L_pred and SIGReg are measured. It is small on purpose: enough capacity to predict across views, little enough to keep the sliced-Gaussian constraint meaningful.

A space can be useful without being a classifier. The projector's job is not to preserve class labels; it is to make views of the same image agree while staying close to N(0, I) in every random slice.

How to read the numbers

Purity is the fraction of the k neighbours whose true label matches the query. Mean cosine is the average similarity of those neighbours; it says how tight the neighbourhood is, not whether it is correct. Vote is the majority label among the neighbours, with ties going to the lower class index.

Raw features have a higher mean cosine almost by construction, so compare purity and vote before comparing similarity. The mean-direction ratio in the table is the norm of the subset mean divided by the mean row norm; near 1 means a dominant shared direction.
