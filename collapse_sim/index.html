What am I looking at?

96 points in three Gaussian clusters, each with two noisy views. The encoder maps a view to a 2-D embedding; the predictor maps one view's embedding to the other. Everything trains in your browser with plain SGD.

encoder 2 → 8 tanh → 2 predictor 2 → 8 tanh → 2 L = L_pred + λ · mean_v SIGReg(z_v)

L_pred is the mean squared error between the predicted and target view embeddings. SIGReg is the same sliced Epps–Pulley statistic used by the lab; its Gaussian null at this sample size is about 1.05.

Why collapse happens

The prediction term is smallest when both views land on the same point. A constant encoder makes L_pred almost zero, and a rank-1 encoder can already predict one coordinate from the other. Nothing in the prediction term asks the embedding to keep its dimensions.

That is why collapse is often dimensional before it is total: the cloud becomes a line, then a point. The loss curve alone can look like progress. The rank and per-dimension spread are the diagnostics that reveal what was lost.

Try this

1. Remove the regularizer. Switch SIGReg off, then replay. L_pred falls towards zero, L_SIGReg explodes and the effective rank drops to about 1. The embedding is now a line.

2. Put it back. Switch SIGReg on with λ = 0.5. The prediction loss settles higher, but the rank stays near 2 and the slices remain close to Gaussian.

3. Find the boundary. Lower λ in steps. At some point the prediction term wins and the cloud collapses again. The regularizer is a constraint, not a metric.

4. Make prediction harder. Raise the view noise. A collapsed encoder still predicts well, so the failure mode does not disappear with harder data.

Toy, not the CIFAR run

This page is a controlled 2-D experiment, not the ViT. The encoder and predictor are tiny, the optimizer is plain SGD, and there is no EMA or probe. The objective is the same shape; the scale is chosen so a collapse fits in a few hundred CPU steps.

Read the rank, not just the loss

The left loss curve rewards prediction. On its own it cannot tell the difference between a representation that encodes the image and one that throws the image away. The geometry panel does: effective rank is the participation ratio of the embedding covariance, so it goes to 1 for a line and 2 for a filled 2-D cloud. The per-dimension σ's show the same thing in the original axes.

In the CIFAR run the projector is 16-d, not 2-d, but the diagnostic is the same: per-dimension spread, correlation and the SIGReg slices. The diagnostics page shows that version.

The two runs

With SIGReg, the objective pays for spread: prediction is a little worse, but every slice stays close to a Gaussian and the rank stays high. Without it, the encoder finds the cheaper solution. The total objective is lower in the collapsed run, which is precisely why the term is needed.

SIGReg is not a monitor bolted onto training; it changes the minimizer. Switch it off and the prediction term is free to choose the constant map.
